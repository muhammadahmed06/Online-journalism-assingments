Karachi Drug Addicts and Infrastructure Theft: What Is Being Done

From Sharae Faisal to Teen Hatti, addicts are stripping steel from Karachi’s roads and bridges. Police drives have sent hundreds to rehab, yet poor lighting and thin patrolling still leave public property exposed.

By Muhammad Ahmed, Naqeeb Ullah, Ali Raza, Shahriyar and Abdul Rafay
Published November 2026. Three-minute read.

KARACHI: Karachi’s drug problem is no longer confined to back lanes. It is now visible in the missing railings, damaged bridges and broken roads of the city’s busiest corridors, according to recent reports by The Express Tribune. The issue has become serious enough that police chiefs, the mayor and residents are all debating how to respond.

A survey published by the newspaper in September 2026 found that drug addicts and petty criminals have stripped railings from major roads, pedestrian bridges, parks and playgrounds. They later turned to flyover pillars and Jersey barriers for steel, taking public infrastructure and other valuables to finance their addiction, the report said. On Sharae Faisal, the theft of steel rods from a bridge left a large pothole and disrupted traffic.

Damage on the ground

In August 2026, The Express Tribune also reported that drug addicts were damaging the historic Teen Hatti bridge. The newspaper’s September report blamed poor street lighting and reduced police patrolling for giving offenders a free hand to steal public property and commit petty crimes. One person quoted in that report argued that the number of addicts involved in such theft is relatively small and should therefore be manageable for the government.

    Crackdowns and rehabilitation

    The authorities have responded with a series of operations. In July 2025, Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab ordered a citywide crackdown after public complaints about stolen iron rods and railings. He directed that addicts found in public spaces be shifted to the City Wardens Headquarters and then to rehabilitation centres. Aaj News reported that City Warden teams were to carry out the process in a secure, humane and structured manner. Mubina Town police detained 13 people in one such drive.

    In March 2026, police in Malir district rounded up addicts on the directives of Sindh IG Javed Alam Odho, in what the report described as an effort to protect the younger generation from drugs; those detained were taken to police stations and briefed on the harms of drug abuse. In May 2026, DIG Asad Raza said more than 480 addicts had been sent to rehabilitation centres during a three-day operation in Pak Colony, Mewa Shah and Jahan Abad, including over 220 on a single day.

    Targeting the supply

    Police have also gone after suppliers. Speaking in May 2026, the Sindh IG said more than 1,000 drug dealers had been arrested in four months under the provincial anti-narcotics policy. Arab News separately reported the arrest of more than 60 suspected traffickers operating near schools, colleges and universities, while AIG Azad Khan has said that the drug network in Karachi is expanding rapidly, particularly around educational institutions.

    Why it matters

    Drug addiction is both a public-health and a public-safety issue. When addicts remove steel from bridges, the cost falls on the city and the risk falls on commuters, as the Sharae Faisal pothole shows. Rehabilitation is the stated aim of the recent drives, but the conditions residents complain about, such as poor lighting and thin patrolling, are ones that no single raid can fix. Whether detention and rehab can deliver lasting results remains an open question.

    Key facts at a glance

    • What: theft of railings, rods and steel from roads, bridges and parks (Express Tribune survey, September 2026).
    • Where: Sharae Faisal, Teen Hatti, Malir, Pak Colony, Mewa Shah, Jahan Abad, Mubina Town.
    • Why (per reports): poor street lighting and reduced police patrolling.
    • Response: city-wide crackdown, rehab shifting, and arrests of dealers and traffickers.
