Karachi Drug Addicts Steal Bridge Steel: What Is Being Done

ExcerptSteel is vanishing from Karachi’s bridges and roads, one railing at a time. Police say they have sent hundreds of addicts to rehab and arrested more than 1,000 dealers. So why is the damage still spreading?

KARACHI: Drug addicts and petty criminals are stripping steel from the city’s roads and bridges, The Express Tribune has reported, and police say they have answered with rehab drives and mass arrests. Railings, steel rods and even flyover pillars have been taken to pay for addiction.

Police say more than 480 addicts were sent to rehab in a three-day operation in May 2026, and the Sindh IG says over 1,000 drug dealers were arrested in four months. Even so, the Tribune’s September report said poor street lighting and thin police patrolling still give thieves a free hand.

The damage

A survey published by the Tribune in September 2026 found railings stripped from major roads, pedestrian bridges, parks and playgrounds. The thieves then moved on to flyover pillars and Jersey barriers, the heavy road dividers, for their steel. On Sharae Faisal, rods stolen from a bridge left a large pothole and slowed traffic.

Steel railings, vanishing one bar at a time.

In August, the Tribune also reported that addicts were damaging the historic Teen Hatti bridge. Its September report blamed poor street lighting and reduced police patrolling for giving offenders a free hand to steal public property and commit petty crimes.

Crackdowns and rehab

The city’s first big move came in July 2025. After complaints about stolen iron rods and railings, Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab ordered a citywide crackdown. Addicts found in public spaces were to be taken to the City Wardens Headquarters and then to rehabilitation centres. Aaj News reported that the wardens were to carry this out in a secure, humane and structured manner. Mubina Town police detained 13 people in one such drive.

    Police took it from there. In March 2026, Malir district police rounded up addicts on the directives of Sindh IG Javed Alam Odho. Those detained were taken to police stations and briefed on the harms of drug abuse. In May, DIG Asad Raza said more than 480 addicts had been sent to rehabilitation centres during a three-day operation in Pak Colony, Mewa Shah and Jahan Abad, including over 220 on a single day.

    Going after the supply

    Police have also gone after the people selling the drugs. Speaking in May 2026, the Sindh IG said more than 1,000 drug dealers had been arrested in four months under the provincial anti-narcotics policy. Arab News separately reported the arrest of more than 60 suspected traffickers operating near schools, colleges and universities. AIG Azad Khan has said the drug network in Karachi is expanding rapidly, particularly around educational institutions. These figures come from officials, and we could not verify them independently.

    Why it matters

    Drug addiction is both a public-health and a public-safety issue. When steel is pulled out of a bridge, the repair bill falls on the city and the risk falls on commuters, as the Sharae Faisal pothole shows. Rehabilitation is the stated aim of the recent drives, but the conditions residents complain about, such as poor lighting and thin patrolling, are not something a single raid can fix.

    One person quoted in the Tribune’s report argued that the number of addicts involved is relatively small. If so, this looks like a problem the city can tackle with steady lighting, patrols and treatment. Whether detention and rehab can deliver lasting results is something the reports we read do not show.

    What we still don’t know

    We went through the coverage listed below and did not find clear answers to these.
    1. How many of the addicts sent to rehab stay off drugs afterwards? The reports count who was sent, not who recovered.
    2. What does it cost the city to replace stolen steel and repair damage like the Sharae Faisal pothole?
    3. Are the street lighting and patrolling gaps named in the Tribune’s September report being fixed, and by whom?

    The short version

    • What: theft of railings, rods and steel from roads, bridges and parks (Express Tribune survey, September 2026).
    • Where: Sharae Faisal, Teen Hatti, Malir, Pak Colony, Mewa Shah, Jahan Abad, Mubina Town.
    • Why, per the reports: poor street lighting and reduced police patrolling.
    • Response: a citywide crackdown, shifting addicts to rehab, and arrests of dealers and traffickers.
